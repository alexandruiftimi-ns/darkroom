# Side B — good

**Thesis in one line:** A coffee brand sold by its bags: four packs thrown across a giant wordmark on the brand's own red.

**Split:** design good · build mixed · technique good — packaging-led and strict; 13 MB of images.

## What works & why

- **Product laid over a giant wordmark** — the bags overlap the letters, so brand and product read as one image → product-over-wordmark → rule: put the product cut-outs on top of the full-width name, with real shadows.
- **The packaging palette is the site palette** — burgundy, kraft and red from the bags become the ground and buttons → product-colour (good/begg-liquid-egg) → rule: sample the page palette from the packaging.
- **Strict shop rows** — each coffee and each merch item sits on cream with name, notes, price and one "+" button → consistent card anatomy → rule: one card shape for every product.
- **One custom face for everything** — the brand's own grotesk carries display and body → one voice → rule: a strong single family beats a pairing when it has the weights.

## What fails & why

- **Image weight** (medium) — over 13 MB of product photography for a short page → asset budget → rule: AVIF, sized per breakpoint; the hero is the only large image.

## Transferable rules

- Do: let the packaging art-direct the page and lay the product over the name.
- Don't: ship full-resolution product shots in every row.

## Never copy

Logo, packaging, photography, exact layout, copy text. Learn the principle only.
