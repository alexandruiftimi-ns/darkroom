## What works & why

- **The rail track as the page's spine** — it ties every section together and literally carries the brand's subject past the reader → motion with meaning (references/motion) → rule: when the business has a physical path (track, road, pipe, river), let scroll travel it.
- **Faded hero film behind dark condensed caps** — the footage is real work, pushed pale so the claim always reads → hero media scrim (references/hero-media) → rule: lighten or darken the clip toward the ground instead of putting a box behind the text.
- **The fact row under the claim** (seat, area, qualification, certificates) — trust before the scroll → rule: B2B heroes earn trust with four checkable facts, not adjectives.
- **Real crew portraits with direct phone numbers** near the end → rule: for a service business, the people who come out are the closing proof.

## What fails & why

- **Two text-heavy middle sections (106 and 121 words)** (medium) → word budget → rule: split long service lists into labelled rows the eye can skim.
- **Pale small-caps labels around 2:1 contrast** (medium) → contrast → rule: tracked labels still need 4.5:1.

## Transferable rules

- Do: make the scroll travel the subject's own route; put checkable facts directly under the claim.
- Don't: let the signature carry the page and then end on a bare form and footer.

## Never copy

Logo, photography, the track-and-train artwork, exact layout and copy. Learn the principle only.
