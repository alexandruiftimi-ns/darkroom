# HomeLab Brand Studio — good

**Thesis in one line:** A logo studio sold the clean, familiar way: one bold claim, one lime action, real brand work right under the fold.

**Split:** design good · build mixed · technique mixed — tidy studio template; lime buttons fail contrast.

**First frame:** warm off-white ground with faint concentric arcs and four tilted tool tiles at the corners; a centred four-line bold claim (30–70 % x, 22–55 % y), a one-line subtitle, a lime and a black button, a handwritten note pointing at the first, a client-logo row at the bottom. Eye: claim → lime button → logos.

## What works & why

- **One lime used only for the main action** → one accent with a job (references/colour) → rule: the brightest colour on the page marks the next step and nothing else.
- **Work thumbnails directly under the hero on phones** → proof before promises → rule: a studio shows its work in the first scroll, before services.
- **A semicircle process diagram instead of a numbered 1-2-3 row** → tells.md (numbered step rows) → rule: give the process a shape that fits it.
- **Handwritten note beside the button** → a human detail at the decision point → rule: one informal touch next to the CTA, not scattered.

## What fails & why

- **Pale text on lime fails contrast** (medium) → contrast → rule: dark ink on light accents; test the button label first.

## Transferable rules

- Do: keep the hero short and show real projects within one scroll.
- Don't: light text on a light accent.

## Never copy

Logo, client marks, project images, exact layout, copy text. Learn the principle only.

**Open question:** the earlier analysis called it the standard studio template ("nothing to learn"); the owner marks it good, and template-like is not a minus. Kept good.
