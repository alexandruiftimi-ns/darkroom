# Designotion — good

**Thesis in one line:** A subscription design studio that says who it serves in two words, "design for", then cycles the audience in a script face.
**Split:** design good · build mixed · technique good — energetic and clear; no h1 and soft contrast underneath.

**First frame:** near-white with a faint lilac haze; small wordmark top-centre; a proof line in a peach pill; centred two-line headline (x 36–64 %, y 30–48 %): a bold geometric "design for" over a large script word that changes ("startups", "brands", "indie hackers"); a navy "See Pricing" button and a quieter "Book a call"; a fan of tilted app and web mockups across the bottom edge; an iridescent star and ribbon in the corners.

## What works & why

- **"design for" + one changing audience word** → principle: kinetic type with meaning (word-swap) → rule: cycle the audience or outcome, keep the verb fixed.
- **Price shown as a single card** ($ per month, what's included, "Book a meeting" beside it) → principle: answer the buying question in one panel → rule: a subscription offer gets one price card, not a table.
- **Real client work as the hero fan and the closing fan** → principle: bookend with proof (wordmark-bookend, used with work) → rule: open and close on the same visual so the page feels complete.

## What fails & why

_Only issues that cost visitors something._

- **No h1 and five unnamed buttons** (medium) — the headline is assembled from animated pieces → semantics law → rule: the animated headline still needs one real h1 underneath.
- **Proof lines at ~3.5:1** (medium) — the "$90M raised" and star-rating lines are faint → contrast law → rule: the proof line is copy, not decoration; give it full contrast.

## Transferable rules

- Do: a fixed verb + a cycling audience word in a contrasting face.
- Don't: build the headline only from animated spans.

## Never copy

Logo, 3D icons, mockups, layout and copy text. Learn the principle only.
