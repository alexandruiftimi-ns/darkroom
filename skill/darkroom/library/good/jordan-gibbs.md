# Jordan Gibbs (portfolio) — good

**Thesis in one line:** A portfolio as a warm still life: an old TV on a wooden sideboard, and every project is a tape you put in.

**Split:** design good · build mixed · technique good — one lit object as the whole menu; phone layout drifts.

**First frame:** a rendered CRT set with chess pieces on top stands centre-low on a dark wood sideboard against a sunlit peach plaster wall; "Pick a tape" in a heavy tight grotesk sits top-centre (y 8–13 %) with one small line ("12 projects · click a tape to play it"); three round play controls sit under the set. The eye goes headline → black screen → play button.

## What works & why

- **One object is the interface** — a TV and tapes turn "browse 12 projects" into a physical choice → borrowed-object metaphor (references/craft) → rule: pick one object the audience already knows how to use and make it the navigation.
- **Light that has a time of day** — low sun and window shadow on plaster make the render read as a photograph → material and light (looks.md) → rule: give a 3D still life one directional light and a real wall, not a void.
- **Six-word first screen** → rule: an exploratory portfolio needs one instruction, not a bio.

## What fails & why

- **The phone layout is not re-composed** (medium) — the set slides off-centre and the tapes clip at the top → rule: re-frame the camera for portrait instead of cropping the desktop shot.

## Transferable rules

- Do: let one familiar object hold the work; light it like a photo.
- Don't: ship the desktop camera to phones.

## Never copy

The set, props, scene and copy. Learn the principle only.
