# SwarmOne (GPU inference tuning) — good

**Thesis in one line:** "More tokens per second" from hardware you already own, said in two colours beside a turning sphere of coral particles.

**Split:** design good · build mixed · technique good — calm one-accent product page; very heavy for a mostly flat page.

**First frame:** off-white ground split by one hairline; left half a four-line claim, two lines black and two coral (x 4–37 %, y 26–60 %), one sentence and a slanted coral button; right half a sphere of coral dot-clusters with a soft floor shadow. The eye goes black line → coral line → sphere.

## What works & why

- **One accent with one job** — coral carries the second half of the claim, the button and the particles, nothing else → colour discipline (references/colour) → rule: one saturated hue, used where the eye should land.
- **The hero object is made of the product's idea** — many small units forming one swarm → rule: an abstract product earns a visual metaphor drawn from its name, not stock 3D.
- **Process told as three tilted cards in depth** with a numbered mono index under them → rule: a three-step process reads better as one moving object than as three equal cards.
- **Short, checkable headlines** ("Same model. Same hardware.") → rule: claims a buyer can test beat adjectives.

## What fails & why

- **Over 18 MB for a page of type and dots** (medium) → page weight → rule: draw particle heroes live or as one short video, never as a stack of heavy media.
- **Coral on cream for small text and buttons** (medium) → contrast → rule: keep the accent for large type; darken it for small labels.

## Transferable rules

- Do: split the claim in two colours: the promise in ink, the twist in the accent.
- Don't: pay megabytes for motion a canvas can draw.

## Never copy

Logo, particle artwork, diagrams and copy. Learn the principle only.
