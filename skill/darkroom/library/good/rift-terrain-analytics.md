# Rift (HALO LAB concept) — good
- **Thesis in one line:** terrain analytics sold as targeting — the page is literally divided by an orange crosshair over aerial relief.
- **First frame:** four quadrants meet at a glowing orange reticle at ~50 % x / 50 % y; top-left giant condensed wordmark, top-right aerial terrain photo cut by a diagonal scan line, lower-left one-sentence claim over a lit rock, lower-right two metric tiles. Eye: wordmark → reticle → claim.
## Signals
| Signal | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | 3 families — ultra-condensed grotesque (display ~400 px) · neo-grotesque (claim ~32 px) · mono caps (nav/labels ~16 px); ~5 sizes |
| Colour · words · weight | disciplined — near-black ground, off-white ink, one orange accent; earth tones only in photos; ~40 words in frame; weight n/a (mockup) |
| Zones · motion · mobile | desktop 4 · mobile not shown; no motion visible · source: estimated from images |
## What works & why
- **Crosshair grid as layout** — the product's verb (locate, target) becomes the page structure → principle: replace tells with an idea from the subject (tells.md, "Why removal isn't enough") → rule: derive the layout primitive from what the product does.
- **Orange only on line, reticle and markers** while the photo carries warm colour → principle: separate UI palette from media palette (color.md rule 1) → rule: take the accent from the imagery's hue but restrict it to chrome and markers.
- **Mono caps reserved for nav and data labels** → principle: a third face only as utility for data (typography.md) → rule: a mono face is acceptable when it only labels data.
## Transferable rules
- Do: let one geometric device (grid line, reticle) tie hero, imagery and data together.
- Don't: add a second accent; the single orange is what makes the reticle readable.

**Open question:** the metric tiles are unsourced numbers (tells.md Proof) and the hero has 4 zones (reading-paths.md rule 2) — a live build needs sources and one fewer zone.
