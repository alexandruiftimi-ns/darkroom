## What works & why

- **Objects as navigation** — chalkboard, letter, cork board and recorder each stand for a section → borrowed art world from the subject's desk → rule: in a personal site, let real objects from the person's life be the menu.
- **Cinematic light** — late sun through one window, warm falloff, one cool accent outside → one light source, one colour story → rule: light the scene once, from one side.
- **The welcome is written into the scene** — the chalkboard is the headline and the instructions ("click around") → copy inside the image world → rule: teach the interaction in the art, not in a tooltip.
- **Polite consent card** — small, cream, equal buttons, out of the focal area → one overlay, not over the subject → rule: consent bottom-centre, ≤ 25 % of the view.

## What fails & why

- **Phones get a "rotate your device" card first** (medium) — a soft gate; the scene is not re-composed for portrait → mobile re-composition → rule: crop or pan the scene for portrait instead of asking people to turn the phone.
- **Generated-interior look** (medium) — the room reads as AI-painted (perfect sunbeam, set-dressed props) → tells: generated imagery → rule: add one real photo or real object of the person so the room is theirs.

## Transferable rules

- Do: make an explorable scene where each object is a section, lit with one source.
- Don't: ask phone visitors to rotate before they see anything.

## Never copy

The room artwork, portrait, objects, exact layout, copy text. Learn the principle only.
