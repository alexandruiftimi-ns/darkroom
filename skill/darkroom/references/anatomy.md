# Anatomy: build the page around the visitor's job

The stock order (hero, logos, three features, testimonial, pricing, CTA, email band) is how software pages look and almost nothing else. Before the storyboard, name the **one job** a visitor comes to do and read its anatomy here: where the action lives, which sections are the category's own, and how the page ends. The style pack dresses the anatomy; the storyboard orders it; neither replaces it.

Every job: the nav carries the job's own action word (Book, Tickets, Shop, Listen, Start free), the hero shows the thing itself (the room, the cover, the dates, the screen), and proof comes in the category's own form, never a generic band of numbers.

**Missing content.** When the brief gives none of a section's content, build a marked sample (dishes typical of the cuisine, track titles) and list it in the handoff. A sample shows the shape of the real thing; it never states a fact a visitor could take as true (a price, a date, a name, a count, a review). A section made only of facts nobody gave (a lineup, a tour) is one line and a TEMP slot, or it waits.

| Job | The action | The category's own sections | How it ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| **subscribe** (newsletter, waitlist, podcast) | in the hero, often the hero itself: name, one line, the email field; a podcast puts the latest episode and its play control under it | a sample or latest issue set as it arrives; who writes it; the archive with dates; the reader count only if given | a second field under the archive. The one job whose close may be an email field |
| **book** (restaurant, class, clinic, studio) | a booking picker (party, date, time) or the next free times as buttons, from the nav and the hero, repeated beside the menu or timetable | the room or the food as a photograph; the menu or the timetable; how to find it; hours and address as their own section | hours, address and the booking again |
| **visit** (shop, gallery, venue, place) | today's hours and the address in the nav or the hero; tickets only if real | what is on now as the lead picture; what is coming as dated rows; the shelves for a shop; getting there | visit information and the address |
| **attend** (event, festival, race, conference) | tickets in the nav from the first screen; the date and the place are the headline | the lineup or speakers with names set large; the days as a schedule; ticket tiers with prices; venue and travel; questions | tickets again, or the questions |
| **buy** (a product, a small shop) | the product with price and options, often as the hero; a buy action after the sections that earn it, or a sticky price bar | the range with prices; the product in use, close; how it is made; delivery and guarantee as plain facts; reviews only if given | the offer again, new arrivals or the guarantee; an email field sits in the footer, not as the close |
| **try** (app, game, desktop tool) | store or download buttons under the headline, one per real platform | the main screen captured from the product; features as named moments shown in screens, not icon tiles; a before and after | the download again beside one last screen. Games are short |
| **sign up** (software, API, developer tool) | start free in the nav and the hero, its terms in one line; for a developer tool the install command may be the action | the product at work (captured, or built when it does not run yet); what it does as named moments; a code sample or integration; pricing; questions | the hero's action again in a short band, or the code sample. Never a newsletter |
| **hire** (studio, freelancer, architect) | a plain contact (email, phone, "Talk to us") in the nav and once after the work | the work as the page: projects (name, client, year, place) with pictures, one opened as a case; what the studio does; where it is | contact details, per office |
| **join** (club, community, membership) | join or "your first visit" in the hero | what a session is like, shown; when and where; membership options with prices; first-visit guidance | the first visit or the options |
| **listen / watch** (band, film, publication) | the new release or issue with its listen, watch or order buttons | the release (cover, tracks or contents); dates as rows with ticket links; earlier work; videos | the dates, or more of the catalogue |

**How pages end,** in the order real sites use them: the hero's action again in a short band; practical details (hours, address, contacts); more of the catalogue; a short trust tail (a guarantee, press, awards the brief gives); a mailing list, for things that recur. The mailing list is one close in five, never the default.

**The mirror test.** Before building, ask: would a neighbouring business in the same category get this exact sequence of sections? If yes, change one decision (the lead composition, the order, how it ends) and say which in the storyboard.
